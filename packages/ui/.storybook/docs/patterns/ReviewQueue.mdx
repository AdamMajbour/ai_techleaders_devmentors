# Review queue

## 1. What it is for

Items that wait for one person's decision. Each item shows everything needed to decide next to the decision itself, says what each choice causes, and says so when it can no longer be decided.

<Canvas of={ReviewQueueStories.EverythingToDecideIsInTheRow} />

## 2. When to use it

- The answers a mentor reads before they reach a seeker (E02-S02).
- Later, any list of items that one person approves or rejects.

## 3. When not to use it, and what to use instead

| Situation | Use instead | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A booking with one clear action | `BookingRow` in a list | Accept and Decline, with the question |
| Many records to compare | List with filters, `Table` | A queue is read and decided one at a time |
| An action that deletes | Confirm destructive action | The risk is a loss, not a decision |

## 4. Anatomy

1. **A short introduction** that says what the items are and why the reader sees them.
2. **The items**, in a `ul`, divided by a hairline (R01.1).
3. **In each item:** what is asked, the content to judge, the context (which note, who else), the deadline, the optional reason, the buttons.
4. **After a decision:** a sentence instead of the buttons.

## 5. Behavior rules

| Rule | Why | Source |
|---|---|---|
| The **whole content to judge** is in the row, not cut | The reader decides on what they have read | D09 (the mentor sees every answer before release), R05 |
| The **consequence of a choice is written next to it** | One rejection rejects the whole answer | Vault spec, Q4 |
| **Who reads the reason** is written next to it | The reader knows the audience | Vault spec (the operator only) |
| **One action runs at a time** per item: both buttons are blocked while it runs | No double decision | Form rules |
| A **refused action says why** and leaves the buttons usable | The reader can act on it | WCAG 3.3.3 |
| A **decided item** shows a sentence, not buttons | The decision is not repeated | Proposal |
| An **expired item** says so and has no buttons. The deadline is the **server's** | The server would refuse the action, and the browser's clock may be wrong | Vault spec |
| Empty, loading and failed states are written, with a retry for the failure | The reader is never left with a blank page | WCAG 3.3.3 |
| The **heading level** of the items fits under the page heading | No gap in the outline | WCAG 1.3.1 |

## 7. Accessibility

- Items are list items. Each has two headings of the same level (the question, the content).
- Buttons have names that tell the items apart: "Approve the answer that cites Ingress upgrades on bare metal".
- An action error is an alert. The reason field has a visible label and a description.

## 8. Content

- **Empty:** "Nothing to review. When an answer cites one of your notes, it appears here and we email you."
- **Refused:** says what happened and whether the reader has to do anything.
- **Deadline:** "Decide by 15:00 (1 h 20 min left)."

## 9. Responsiveness

One column, at most the width of `max-w-session`. At 320 px long titles and names wrap and nothing is cut.

## 10. Code

```tsx
<ul>
  {items.map((item) => (
    <ReviewItem key={item.answerId} item={item} now={serverTime} headingLevel={2} onApprove={approve} onReject={reject} />
  ))}
</ul>
```

## 11. Do and don't

| | Example | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Do | "If you reject, the whole answer is rejected and the seeker is refunded." next to Reject | The reader knows what the click does |
| Don't | Approve and Reject with no explanation | One click, an unknown result |
| Do | "The time to decide has passed." with no buttons | The reader is not offered what the server refuses |
| Don't | A countdown that ticks on the page | Announced every second, and it follows the browser's clock |
