## Decision summary

Founder A chose the first build: paid 25/50-minute text sessions plus a vault answer drawn from the mentor's notes, with no subscription for the seeker (D01, D02). The agent had recommended sessions alone with the vault later; the choice stands as made. Implementation readiness was withheld until the decisions below; on 2026-10-25 founder A signed the Definition of Ready for the pilot (D23), which accepts risk and is not evidence. On 2026-10-25 founder A decided, and reported founder B's confirmation of D10 and D14 and of the mentor interviews (D20): mentors are found through a list filtered by technology plus their own links (D04); the primary metric is prepaid bookings, with five stop-or-revise criteria with provisional thresholds and a time-to-booking measure (D05, D08); the vault answer is generated by AI from the mentor's notes and shows the mentor's name and the source note's title, the mentor is told when publishing notes and sees every answer before release until the pilot ends (D18: until 2026-12-14), and earns 80% of its price, split equally among the mentors of cited notes, who each see the answer before release (D06, D09, D10, D11, D14); the pilot is planned for 2026-11-02 to 2026-12-14 for invited participants at 25 EUR per 25-minute session and 10 EUR per vault answer, paid by the seeker (D16, D18, D19); payouts and refunds go through Stripe, with refunds approved by founder A (D17); Now is the pilot with real money and personal data of people outside the founders, under minimal terms set by founder A without legal review (D12, D15), while the staff engineer's listing question (Q09) blocks a public list; R03, R04 and R05 are active, accepted untested (D07).

Who has the problem: developers hit a concrete blocker with nobody to ask. Three of four seekers described a recent incident costing half a day to about a week; on one Discord 21% of help threads got no human reply in two weeks and 9 of 31 urgent threads got none (see Problems). Three mentors answer the same questions unpaid in DMs and Slack.

The mismatch most likely to change the choice: seekers describe wanting a quick answer from someone who did the same thing, over text, within the hour or the same day. Nobody asked for saved or reusable answers and three declined a subscription; the vault is backed only by mentors' own use of their notes (A01). The one-hour promise also depends on mentors answering that fast, which no source tests (A02); the mentor's preview of every vault answer in the pilot (D09) adds mentor time before an answer reaches the seeker. Two seekers set an effort limit: the fintech developer wants someone within 20 minutes with no account setup and rolls back if booking takes over five minutes, and the platform developer needs someone found in five minutes; prepayment and mentor acceptance (R04, R05) are now active but remain untested against that (D07). Prices also diverge: 31 of 54 survey respondents would pay up to 25 EUR or nothing, while the staff engineer wants a high price as a filter (Q05). Willingness to pay is stated (37 of 54 in a survey) but only 3 of 54 had paid someone the last time they were stuck, and nobody has committed anything (A03). Survey answers do not fully agree with each other: 17 would pay nothing in one question, 9 would not pay in another.

Open: the verifier's review of the pilot terms (D15, D17), which has not taken place and gates the first real payment; terms of service for release (Q07); Q09, the staff engineer's view of a listing, which blocks only a public list; the date of the A01 and Q08 interviews, led by founder A (D23). The entity is registered and the verifier legally qualified on the owner's word (D22), and founder B's confirmations of D08, D10, D14 and D20 are as reported by founder A. The thresholds, windows, owners, prices, the mentor's 80% share and the pilot terms are provisional targets or choices with no baseline for the thresholds, no mentor input and no legal review; payment fees, the 50-minute price and the A05 threshold are not set.

Test design: the commitment test counts prepaid bookings apart from stated intent and records the time from landing to a prepaid booking, so the effort limits of the fintech and platform developers are observed rather than assumed (A03, D05). The vault test has two steps: whether three mentors' notes answer ten real recent questions, which gates mentor supply, and whether seekers book or pay for such an answer at a separate price, which can narrow D01 (A01, D05). The A01 test uses mentor-written answers, not the AI-generated one chosen in D06, so answer quality and mentors' acceptance of the AI route remain untested. If mentors' own links bring too few bookings, founder A decides afterwards whether to add search or change scope (A05, D04). The A05 count has no threshold; founder A decides after the result (D13).

Next action: the two behavioural tests in the collection plan, one for sessions and one for the vault answer, which separate words from bookings and payments. Owners and review dates are assigned in D08 (2026-11-30 and 2026-12-14); founder B's acceptance was reported by founder A and not captured in founder B's own words.

## Problems, with evidence

- Three of four seekers described a recent blocker with a cost: half a day and a workaround they do not trust; a release slipped from Monday to Tuesday; about a week of unpaid time after an estimate came out too low. `[INTERVIEW]` `2026-08-20-interview-working-dev.md`, `2026-09-01-interview-backend-dev-fintech.md`, `2026-09-02-interview-freelancer-react.md`
- Their workaround was search, a community Discord or Slack, which gave late, contradictory or wrong answers ("it depends" and a talk link; a wrong answer next day); the one paid attempt (Codementor) took 40 minutes to find someone. `[INTERVIEW]` `2026-09-01-interview-backend-dev-fintech.md`, `2026-09-02-interview-freelancer-react.md`, `2026-08-20-interview-working-dev.md`
- In 14 days on one Discord (about 4,000 members), 109 help threads; 23 (21%) never got a human reply; for the other 86 the median first reply took 67 minutes (recomputed from the CSV; the data note says 68); 31 threads (28%) carried an urgency signal and 9 of them got no reply. `[DATA]` `2026-09-04-data-request-discord-help.md` and `2026-09-04-discord-help-requests.csv`, 2026-08-18 to 2026-08-31, six `#help-*` channels, bot and job posts removed
- In the same Discord's survey 30 of 54 had been stuck on something specific with nobody to ask this week or this month, 38 asked an AI assistant, 31 a community, and 3 paid someone, all about the last time they were stuck. `[DATA]` `2026-09-04-survey-discord-willingness-to-pay.md`, 2026-08-29 to 2026-09-03
- Mentors answer the same few questions unpaid and paste from a private doc: monthly and costing "two evenings a month" for one; weekly, ten minutes each, for another; the staff engineer answers only the "interesting" ones. `[INTERVIEW]` `2026-08-22-interview-mentor.md`, `2026-09-03-interview-senior-python-meetups.md`, `2026-09-03-interview-staff-engineer-declined-before.md`
- How often a given seeker gets stuck is not established: "ciagle" was never quantified and the fintech case was one Friday; that interview did not ask about pasting production yaml to a stranger. `[INTERVIEW]` `2026-09-02-interview-freelancer-react.md`, `2026-09-01-interview-backend-dev-fintech.md`
- The seekers' desired outcome differs by person: the working developer wants someone who did the same thing to look at the code "within the hour"; the fintech developer wants someone within twenty minutes, with no account setup, and rolls back if booking takes over five minutes; the freelancer wants it the same day, text for a bug, and the missing point named; the platform developer would pay only for a rare one-off, found in five minutes and done within the hour, on the company. `[INTERVIEW]` `2026-08-20-interview-working-dev.md`, `2026-09-01-interview-backend-dev-fintech.md`, `2026-09-02-interview-freelancer-react.md`, `2026-09-02-interview-platform-dev-solves-alone.md`

## Goals and success criteria

- Primary metric, baseline, threshold, date: the number of people who prepay a 25-minute text session in the test window, counted apart from stated intent; launch state 0 (nobody has booked or paid), with no baseline behind the threshold; provisional threshold fewer than 5 people within 4 weeks of pilot start, planned for 2026-11-02, review 2026-11-30 (D08). `[DOCUMENT]` `decisions/D05-primary-metric-and-stop-criteria.md`; thresholds `[DOCUMENT]` `decisions/D08-thresholds-windows-and-test-owners.md`

## Kill criteria

Founder A accepted the structure (D05) and, on 2026-10-25, the provisional values below (D08); they are targets with no baseline and the owner may edit them. Planned pilot start 2026-11-02.

- Fewer than 5 people prepay within 4 weeks of pilot start: stop building checkout and talk to those who declined (A03). `[DOCUMENT]` `decisions/D08-thresholds-windows-and-test-owners.md`
- No invited mentor with a warm contact publishes availability within 3 weeks of the invitation, with first invitations planned for 2026-11-02: change how supply is found before building for seekers (A04). `[DOCUMENT]` `decisions/D08-thresholds-windows-and-test-owners.md`
- The median reply to paid test questions is longer than 60 minutes: change the promise from "within the hour" to "the same day" (A02). `[DOCUMENT]` `decisions/D08-thresholds-windows-and-test-owners.md`
- The mentors' notes answer fewer than 6 of 10 real recent questions: seek other mentors with notes instead of building the vault from these; a gate on supply, not a test of seeker demand (A01). `[DOCUMENT]` `decisions/D08-thresholds-windows-and-test-owners.md`
- Fewer than 3 people book or pay for a vault answer at a separate price within 4 weeks: D01 narrows to sessions only (A01). `[DOCUMENT]` `decisions/D08-thresholds-windows-and-test-owners.md`
- Optional measure, not a stop criterion: a time from landing to a prepaid booking above 5 minutes, the fintech developer's own limit, counts against R04 and R05 when the result is read (A03). `[DOCUMENT]` `decisions/D08-thresholds-windows-and-test-owners.md`
